Teaching a computer to route like an experienced driver

Navigation apps suggest the shortest route. Experienced delivery drivers do something smarter, because they know their streets. This app shows an AI model that learns their habits and predicts the order they would deliver in.

What it is

A study of how real drivers plan their day

A delivery van leaves the depot with around 150 packages. In what order should the driver deliver them? The shortest path looks right on paper, but skilled drivers often choose differently: they know where parking is easy, which streets jam, and which areas are best handled together. This project uses thousands of real routes to learn those choices.

6,112 real routes learned from 5 US cities ~150 stops per route Data from the Amazon Last Mile Routing Research Challenge

What it does

Predicts the route a skilled driver would take

Takes a day's deliveries

It starts from the same information a driver has in the morning: the list of stops and how long it takes to drive between them.

Predicts the driver's order

It proposes the order in which an experienced driver would visit every stop, not just the order that looks shortest on a map.

Shows how close it gets

It compares its prediction with what the driver really did, and with the plain shortest route, so you can see the difference yourself.

How it does it

Three steps, from past routes to a new plan

Every address belongs to a small delivery area. Drivers finish one area before starting the next, so the key is getting the order of the areas right.

1

Learn the drivers' habits

The model studies past routes and learns which area drivers usually go to next, where they tend to start, and where they finish.

2

Choose the order of areas

For a new route, it picks the order of delivery areas that best matches those habits.

3

Order every stop

Inside each area it finds the shortest path through the stops, giving one complete route from depot to depot.

How close does it get to real drivers?

The model was tested on routes it had never seen, and compared with the plain shortest route.

How to read the score. Every predicted route gets a score that measures how different it is from the route the driver really took. 0 means identical, and lower is better. For comparison, visiting the stops in random order scores about 0.9. It is the official score of the Amazon challenge.
